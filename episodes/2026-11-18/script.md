# Fed Finds $3 Trillion in Off-Book AI Commitments, Funds Cut Tech | OWITH Daily

Good morning from OWITH.ai, the briefing that gives you only what's important to hear in AI and tech. It's Wednesday, November 18.

Today, the Federal Reserve takes its first close look at the money behind the AI buildout, and fund managers cut their tech bets by the most in twenty years.

We start with the Fed. Fortune reports that the central bank's November Financial Stability Report includes, for the first time, a section on leverage in the data center buildout. It finds more than three trillion dollars of AI related obligations that do not show up as debt for firms like Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Nvidia and Oracle, mostly signed leases that have not yet started, take or pay contracts and residual value guarantees. Fed Governor Michael Barr warned that this opacity can make selloffs worse, because it is hard to know where the risk sits. The Fed still calls the banking system sound. For leaders, the real financial footprint of the AI buildout is far larger than the reported debt suggests.

Investors are already moving. Bank of America's November Global Fund Manager Survey, reported by Fortune, shows cut their technology holdings by the largest amount since June two thousand six. Fifty two percent expect at least one AI hyperscaler to announce a cut in capital spending within six months, and only sixteen percent want companies to spend more, the lowest share since May twenty twenty. Wall Street forecasts still point the other way. Goldman Sachs projects hyperscaler spending rising fifty eight percent next year, to about one point four trillion dollars, and estimates that the AI boom has lifted S and P five hundred earnings twenty five percent above trend. The gap between investor mood and corporate plans is the number to watch.

In life sciences, Isomorphic Labs has raised five billion dollars in a Series C, CNBC reports. Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital co led the round, with Andreessen Horowitz and Alphabet also investing. The company, spun out of Google DeepMind in twenty twenty one and run by Demis Hassabis, raised a two point one billion dollar Series B only in May. Hassabis says the money will build foundational biology models and move the first drug programs toward clinical trials. Dealroom data cited by CNBC shows drug discovery startups raised a record twenty three point two billion dollars this year. AI drug discovery is now funded at the scale of frontier labs, and the next test is results in the clinic.

OpenAI is spreading its chip bets. Fortune's Eye on AI newsletter reports that OpenAI has named TensorWave as the first neocloud provider for the six gigawatt AMD deal it signed in October. TensorWave will supply four hundred fifty seven megawatts across several US sites, about seven and a half percent of the deal, with capacity expected in the first half of twenty twenty seven. OpenAI is also designing its own chip with Broadcom, called Jalapeno, which is set to scale production next year. A source told Fortune that OpenAI's models still run almost entirely on Nvidia hardware, while Anthropic already spreads its work across four chip suppliers. With Nvidia's leadership in turmoil, a second source of compute has become a strategic priority.

Coding agents are getting managers. Cognition, valued at forty eight billion dollars, has launched Devin Bots, Fortune reports in an exclusive. A Bot joins a team's Slack, keeps context for weeks, splits a project into tasks and hands them to Devin agents, using cheaper models for routine code. Cognition says its annualized revenue passed one billion dollars in September, and Citigroup uses Devin with fourteen thousand engineers. The launch puts Cognition up against OpenAI's Dots and Anthropic's Duet, built by the same labs that supply its models. For engineering leaders, the thing being bought is shifting from a coding assistant to a supervised team of agents.

Finally, an update on the story we covered on Sunday. Jensen Huang has agreed to closed door interviews with three House panels, Fortune reports: Foreign Affairs, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and Intelligence. Huang appears on December first, and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter on December second. The chairmen had warned they would subpoena Huang if he did not come voluntarily. Huang said on Tuesday he would be honored to return as chief executive, but a person familiar with the matter told CNBC that the board is not considering it. The testimony will shape how Washington judges Nvidia's export compliance.

The thread today is risk: the AI buildout has grown big enough that central bankers, fund managers and Congress all want to know who carries it.
