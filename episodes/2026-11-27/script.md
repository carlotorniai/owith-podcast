# Open Weight AI Wins Business, Qualcomm Plans Life After Apple | OWITH Daily

Good morning from OWITH.ai, the briefing that gives you only what's important to hear in AI and tech. It's Friday, November 27.

Today, businesses move their AI spending toward cheaper open weight models, and Qualcomm plans for life after the iPhone.

We start with where companies actually send their AI work. Fortune reports that open weight models, the kind a business can download and customize, now carry a majority of real traffic on at least one major platform. Vercel says fifty six percent of tokens routed through its AI gateway in August went to open weight models, up from seven percent a year earlier. Pinterest uses Alibaba's Qwen models and reports cutting inference costs by more than ninety percent. Why it matters: AI agents can burn about five times the tokens of a chatbot, so cost now drives model choice. The catch is politics. Many leading open models are Chinese, and Reuters has reported that Washington is weighing a rule requiring companies to disclose their use of them.

Next, a chipmaker rebuilding after losing its biggest showcase. Fortune reports that the iPhone 18, released in September, is the first Apple flagship to run on Apple's own modem, ending roughly fifteen years of Qualcomm chips inside nearly every iPhone. Qualcomm's revenue for the fiscal year to September fell three percent to forty three point one billion dollars. Chief marketing officer Don McGuire says the company now pushes into laptops, cars and data centers, with Meta and Amazon Web Services signed as data center customers and a target of five billion dollars in data center revenue in 2027. The lesson for any supplier is clear. Dependence on one giant customer is a risk you eventually pay for.

OpenAI's hardware ambitions got an outside explanation this week. Speaking to Fortune at Web Summit in Lisbon, Tony Fadell, the former Apple executive behind the iPod, said Sam Altman needs Jony Ive to build a device because Apple will never hand OpenAI the context its sensors collect. In his view, OpenAI has to recreate the microphones, cameras and location data an iPhone already holds, perhaps in a pendant, pen or speaker. Altman frames it differently, saying AI simply wants a new form factor. Either way, the fight over AI assistants is turning into a fight over who owns the device and the data around you.

Reddit is making its case to investors. Its shares are down sixty two percent this year, and Fortune notes they fell from just under one hundred thirty dollars to just under ninety one after the October earnings call, when chief executive Steve Huffman said Google's AI features were cutting search referrals. Chief operating officer Jen Wong told Fortune the answer is loyal, logged in users. People who spend about five minutes a day on Reddit in their first year spend about forty minutes by their fifth. That loyalty now matters more than search traffic for any publisher.

In health care, an unusual request from the vendors themselves. In a Fortune commentary, Chris Altchek, chief executive of the clinical AI company Cadence, and Adam Boehler, a former senior Medicare official, argue that Medicare should pay AI enabled care companies less. They say AI so far mostly helps hospitals bill more and insurers pay less, a cost that lands on patients. Their proposal is a five year contract with prices falling every year and twenty percent of payment tied to outcomes, starting with heart failure, which affects more than seven million Americans. Why it matters: if regulators adopt the idea, AI savings in health care would flow to payers and patients, and vendors would compete on results.

Finally, funding. Every, the AI focused media and software company founded by Dan Shipper, raised seventeen and a half million dollars at a one hundred ten million dollar valuation, in a round led by IA Ventures, Fortune reports. It has almost ten thousand paying subscribers. In the same report, Salt Lake City's Leash Biosciences raised eleven million dollars to generate lab data that trains and tests AI for drug discovery. Small checks, but they show investors still pay for proprietary audiences and proprietary data.

The thread today is price and control: AI keeps getting cheaper to run, and the winners are the ones who own the customer, the device or the data.
