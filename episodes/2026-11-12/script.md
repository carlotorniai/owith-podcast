# Anthropic's $3.4B Gas Power Deal, Google's Gemini for Healthcare | OWITH Daily

Good morning from OWITH.ai, the briefing that gives you only what's important to hear in AI and tech. It's Thursday, November 12.

Today we start with Anthropic buying its own power supply, then look at Google's push to take the paperwork out of American healthcare.

Fortune reports that Anthropic is committing three point four billion dollars to gas fired generators for its data centers. The seller is OnePWR Solutions, an equipment supplier based in San Antonio, and the package covers one point seven gigawatts of heavy duty generators, plus the teams to install them. Anthropic takes over generator orders OnePWR had placed with Caterpillar, and the units should be available within two years. The Financial Times calls it the largest direct investment in power generation equipment by an AI company. Why it matters: Anthropic's IPO filing lists about nine hundred billion dollars in computing and infrastructure commitments, and warns about volatile gas prices and competition for pipeline capacity. Power has become a strategic asset, and the AI labs want to own it.

Google Cloud has launched Gemini for Healthcare, Fortune reports. The product connects to hospital and insurer records and billing systems, including Epic. It summarizes patient charts, flags gaps and conflicts, prepares prior authorizations and estimates what patients will pay, citing a source for each finding. It can read records but cannot change them. Launch customers include Northwell Health, HCA Healthcare, Optum and Highmark Health. Northwell's chief executive, John D'Angelo, says his system runs on a one percent margin at best, and it has about four hundred people working on insurance denials. With administrative costs estimated at a quarter to a third of American health spending, the first big payoff from AI in medicine may come from the back office.

Agents for everyday errands are off to a slow start. Fortune gathered the numbers from third quarter earnings calls. Uber's finance chief said AI agents booked just four rides and five hundred food deliveries during Halloween week, out of three hundred twenty million trips and orders. DoorDash chief executive Tony Xu counted maybe several hundred agent orders since launch. Airbnb's Brian Chesky called agent bookings a very non material number against nearly half a million bookings a day. Uber notes that customers need a median of fifty seconds to order a ride. For companies betting on agentic commerce, consumers still trust their own thumbs for quick purchases.

In Washington, the Congressional Budget Office estimates the federal deficit reached four hundred nine billion dollars in October, the first month of fiscal twenty twenty seven, Fortune reports. That works out to more than one hundred billion dollars of borrowing a week, and a gap one hundred twenty five billion dollars larger than a year earlier. The budget office blames much of the jump on timing, because some payments due in November were made in October. Still, the full fiscal year twenty twenty six deficit came to one point nine trillion dollars, or six point two percent of GDP, with interest on the debt of one point one four trillion. A government borrowing at this pace competes for the same capital that funds the AI buildout.

Robots need to see how people use their hands, and one startup is paying workers to show them. Fortune reports that Memories.ai, founded by former Meta researchers, is launching Memories Upload, a global gig platform for recording first person video. Contributors wear a small camera called the LUCI Pin on the head, chest or wrist while they prepare food, stock shelves or repair equipment. The company says its network logged more than one million hours of footage in its first month of beta, and it aims for ten million hours by March twenty twenty seven. It says three of the top five frontier AI labs are customers, and it works with Nvidia. Training data for physical AI is turning into a labor market of its own.

Finally, retail. J. Crew has launched Style Advisor, an AI shopping assistant designed to think like a stylist, Fortune reports. It draws on thousands of outfit pairings from the brand's photo shoots and asks shoppers clarifying questions about the occasion. The tool runs on several Anthropic models. Fable five point one handles the harder styling judgments, while smaller models keep the conversation quick. McKinsey data cited by Fortune shows fifty six percent of consumers use AI chat or search tools, but only twenty seven percent use assistants built by retailers. That is the gap J. Crew wants to close.

The thread today is ownership, as AI companies and their customers race to control the power, the data and the relationship with the buyer.
